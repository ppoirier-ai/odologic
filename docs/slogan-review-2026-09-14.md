# Slogan review, 2026-09-14

Candidate under review:

  "Forget Large Language Models (LLMs), welcome to Large Decision Graph (LDGs)"

Verdict: keep the contrast instinct, discard the line. Do not run it on investors.

## What works

1. The "Large X Y" cadence is instant placement. An investor hears a category,
   not a feature, in six words, and the acronym does the explaining.
2. "Decision" is the right noun. It points at judgment, adjudication, standing,
   which is what the ledger actually holds. It is closer to the product than
   "language" is.
3. It is memorable. That is not a small thing, most of the existing copy
   (landing-copy.md, README) is mechanism prose with no tag line at all.

## Why it fails

1. It contradicts our own architecture and answers a question we already field
   badly. The FAQ says an LLM can propose claims but never certify its own
   proposals, and the strategy doc says MCP integration, not a competing chat
   harness, so labs' harness investment becomes our distribution. A slogan that
   opens by telling buyers to forget LLMs fights the distribution plan and
   invites the first question a technical investor will ask: so you do not use
   models at all? We lose that exchange for no gain.
2. "Large" claims size in the one dimension where we lose. The graph is
   local first, SQLite, per user, deliberately sparse. Nobody's decision graph
   beats a training corpus on scale. The scarce goods named in the strategy doc
   are provenance and calibration, not size. The adjective undersells the moat
   and picks a fight we cannot win.
3. "Decision graph" is taken. The OMG DMN standard defines the decision
   requirements graph (DRG) and the decision requirements diagram (DRD), used by
   Camunda, Drools, and Red Hat Process Automation Manager. LDG reads as a
   rebrand of enterprise rule engines, which is the opposite of novel.
4. It carries no mechanism. Claim, evidence, defeat, permanence. None of that
   survives in "decision graph". An investor still cannot say what they bought.
5. Negation led slogans position us as the anti incumbent. Most investors in the
   room are long LLMs. "Forget X" reads as naive or as a feature a frontier lab
   ships in a minor release.

## What I would run instead

Tier 1, mechanism forward, uses our own thesis line:

  LLMs hold conclusions. Odologic holds the argument.

Tier 2, buyer consequence, for decks and cold intros:

  Language models propose. The ledger decides.

Tier 3, cheapest to ship, it already exists in our copy:

  Nothing stands on fluency alone.

If the acronym contrast is genuinely wanted for one slide, use it as a
contrast panel, never as the position, and expand it honestly:

  LLM, generates the plausible. LDG, records what stands.
  Ledgered decision graph: every claim traced, every defeat kept.

"Ledgered" keeps the ledger metaphor that the name and the object model already
carry, and it says permanence instead of size.

## Test to apply to any future line

Does a non technical investor learn who buys this and what changes for them?
If not, it is a tag line, not the position. Our best existing sentence already
passes: every claim shows its evidence, or it does not stand.
