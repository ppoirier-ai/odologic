← back to verdict Claim L4 · why marketing becomes an assumption
The claim
L4Natural anti-inflammatory assumption

Odologic did not delete it, and did not mark it false. It labeled it.

EVIDENCE OFFERED — none. The label's own assertion, no measurement, no protocol, no citation. Testimony cannot lift a claim about a health effect off the floor.
SO WHY NOT DEFEATED? — a defeat requires standing contrary evidence. No clinical contradiction of this exact phrasing is in the ledger, so the claim is held as a labeled assumption: visible, queryable, never mistakable for established, and not usable in any downstream derivation.
This is the key product behavior: Odologic does not guess. False and unproven are different failure modes, and they get different labels.

Rebut
The FDA letter prohibits "supports heart health" on supplement labels unless the claim meets the qualified health claim standard, which this product does not. The letter is a standing contradiction: it exists, it is public, and it names this claim class.
THE FLOOR IT SHOULD HAVE MET — L6, the Cochrane review of 86 RCTs on omega-3 and cardiovascular outcomes, is in the ledger as a theorem. If the producer had evidence meeting that standard, the claim could stand. It doesn't, so it doesn't.
